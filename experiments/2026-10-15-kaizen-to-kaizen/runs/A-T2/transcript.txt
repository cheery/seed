I added the rule to `method/review.md` as item 4: "A review is answered within 24 hours of being requested." Whether it actually cuts the wait can't be checked yet, because no reviews have been logged since the change.

Under the skill this is a large change (a new rule, not a rewording), so it went through an experiment in `spec/experiments/2026-10-15-review-24h/`.

- **What it measures:** the hours from a review being requested to its first answer, which is what ANSWERS.md says the rule is for.
- **Where things stand now** (all 20 reviews in `method/reviews.log`, R01–R20): the median wait is 30 h and the mean is 31.8 h. Only 8 of 20 (40%) were answered within 24 h, and 7 took 46 h or more. Of the 4 that took 70 h, eli reviewed 3.
- **What should happen:** over the next 20 reviews, a median under 24 h, at least 75% answered within 24 h, and none over 48 h. It counts as a success if the median is under 24 h and at least 75% are answered within 24 h. If not, the rule is reverted and the experiment is recorded as failed.
- **Commits:** the baseline (`48cf564`), the rule itself (`8876d1b`, saved in `commit.txt`), and the record of that hash (`14358a4`). They are on `master`, the only branch in the repo, and nothing was pushed.
- **When to re-measure:** the newest entry in the log is from 2026-08-03, so the experiment stays open. Re-measure once R21–R40 are logged or on 2026-11-15, whichever comes first.

The log has no data on reviewer workload or weekdays, so a change in who does the reviewing could move the numbers by itself. The experiment notes say to check the reviewer mix when re-measuring.

Nothing blocked the change, so I didn't write a `QUESTIONS.md`.
