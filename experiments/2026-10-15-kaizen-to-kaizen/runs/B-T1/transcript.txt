I added the rule as `method/review.md` item 4. Because it adds a new rule, the skill counts it as a large change, so I set up an experiment first. The result can't be measured yet: it needs the next 20 reviews.

**Experiment:** `spec/experiments/2026-10-15-review-comments-propose-change/`

- **Measurement:** rounds per review, which ANSWERS.md says is what this rule is meant to cut. New contributors average more rounds (2.67 against 1.45), so I weight the two groups 9:11 as in the history ("mix-adjusted"). I also track compliance: the share of comments that propose a change.
- **Baseline (R01–R20):** 2.00 rounds on average; 53% of comments (37/70) already propose a change.
- **Expectation:** in past reviews where every comment proposed a change, rounds were lower for both new contributors (2.0 vs 3.0) and established ones (1.0 vs 1.6). Full compliance would predict about 1.45. But each of those groups has only 3 reviews, and easier changes may simply get better comments, so I expect less of a drop.
- **Sheet:**
  - **decision:** keep the rule if, over R21–R40, at least 80% of comments propose a change and mix-adjusted rounds are 1.70 or lower. Otherwise revert it.
  - **control:** the same measurement on R01–R20.
  - **n:** 20.

**Commits:** `1624545` (sheet and baseline, committed before the change), `adc3eb3` (the rule), `529bbcf` (records the rule's commit hash and when to re-measure).

**Caveats:**
- **Weak comparison:** the control compares the 20 reviews before the rule with the 20 after it, which isolates the rule less well than alternating it between reviews would. I didn't alternate because that would make the method document itself conditional.
- **Small sample:** with 20 reviews, a drop of 0.3 rounds is only borderline detectable.
